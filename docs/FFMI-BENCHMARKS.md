# FFMI gym-goer comparison

The chart's **Average gym-goer (sample)** point is a training-sample reference, not a
representative estimate of every gym member. It changes with the saved sex model.
No source links or study names are rendered in the app's help.

- **Men: 22.1.** Rounded from 22.09, the reported FFMI mean for 248 men who trained
  with weights at least twice weekly. Their mean age was about 26, with roughly five
  years of training. Body measurements were self-reported; some participants
  reported performance-enhancing drug use. This is not a drug-free norm.
  [Original research, table 1 and methods](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01992/full).
- **Women: 20.6 on the app's scale.** The reported unadjusted mean was 16.7 in 47
  resistance-trained women, measured using DXA including bone mineral content.
  Their mean age was about 20. The app divides female FFMI by 0.81, so the plotted
  comparison is `round(16.7 / 0.81, 1) = 20.6`, rather than plotting the raw 16.7
  against adjusted scores.
  [Original conference abstract, number 756, page 162](https://sports.vtcus.com/docs/default-source/event-documents/annual-meeting-documents/2018-minneapolis-mn/full-2018-abstract-file.pdf?sfvrsn=e25d42e2_2)
  ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1249/01.mss.0000535621.14086.ab)).

These are separate convenience samples with different measurement methods and
training histories. Their difference does not quantify a sex effect. Neither point
is a personal target, genetic limit, health threshold, or estimate of drug use.
Body-fat measurement error also changes the user's calculated score. The existing
model reference and other historical comparison points retain their formulas.

The plotted point and legend are qualified as a sample average; the existing
methodology popup explains the adjustment and limitations. Numeric precision is
display rounding, not evidence that a population average is known to one decimal.
